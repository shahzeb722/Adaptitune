## Invitation

We invite you to take part in a study about software that corrects the pitch of a singing voice. Please read this sheet before you decide. Ask us about anything that is not clear, and take as long as you need.

## Who is doing the study?

Three final-year BS Computer Science students at COMSATS University Islamabad, Lahore Campus, as their final year project: Muhammad Shahzeb Khan, Haris Khan and Afzaal Afzal. The supervisor is Ms. Humera Faisal.

## What is the study about?

Pitch-correction software ("auto-tune") nudges sung notes towards the right pitch. Most of it uses the same settings for everyone. Our program, AdaptiTune, learns which settings each singer prefers from what they keep and what they discard. We want to find out whether singers like its versions better than a fixed setting and our default setting, and how they compare with settings singers choose themselves.

## Do I have to take part?

No. Taking part is entirely voluntary. You can stop a session, or leave the study, at any time without giving a reason. Saying no or leaving has no effect on your studies, your grades, or how anyone at the university treats you.

## Who can take part?

Anyone aged 18 or over who sings at any level, from beginner to trained, and can come to five short sessions on different days. You should not know of any hearing loss. Members of the AdaptiTune team cannot take part.

## What will I do?

There are **five sessions, each at most 20 minutes, on different days within about four weeks**, in a quiet room on campus. In each session you will:

1. **Sing three short phrases** of 10 to 20 seconds each, without music, into a microphone. Any song you like will do.
2. **Listen to twelve corrected versions** of your phrases through headphones. For each one you answer two questions: would you keep this version, and how natural does it sound, on a scale from 1 to 5. There are no right or wrong answers. We want your own taste. Neither you nor the researcher is told which method made which version.
3. **Take a short break** halfway through.
4. **Set the correction yourself** with four sliders for up to five minutes, until it sounds right to you, and then rate your own version.

## What is recorded?

* Your three sung phrases from each session, as audio files.
* Your answers, how long each answer took, and the settings you chose.

There is no video, and we do not record anything else. Your name is never stored with your recordings or answers. Everything is filed under a code, such as P07.

## Are there any risks?

The risks are small. Listening for a while can be tiring, so each session is short, has a break, and keeps the volume at a comfortable level. Some people feel shy singing in front of others. Only one researcher is in the room, and you can choose what to sing. If you feel uncomfortable at any point, tell the researcher and the session stops.

## Are there any benefits?

There is no direct benefit to you. You help us find out whether software that adapts to its user serves singers better. [Payment or token of thanks: to be decided before the application, for example none, or refreshments.]

## How is my information kept safe?

* Recordings and answers are kept only on the project's laptop, which is encrypted, and never uploaded to the internet. AdaptiTune itself makes no internet connections.
* The list that links your name to your code is kept apart from the study data, in a locked drawer or one encrypted file, and only the study team can see it.
* Only the three students and the supervisor can see the study data.
* Recordings are deleted when the final project report has been assessed, by [date, about March 2028], unless you agree on the consent form that we may keep them for the project's later evaluation. Even then they are deleted by [date].
* The report and any research paper show only results for the whole group or for coded participants, such as "P07 kept 8 of 12 versions". They never include names or recordings.

## What if I want to withdraw?

Tell any member of the team, at any time, without giving a reason. We will delete your recordings, your answers and everything the program learned about you. Results that are already part of the written analysis can be removed until [date, when the analysis is written up, about December 2027]. After that date your recordings are still deleted, but the group results that include your answers stay as they are.

## What happens to the results?

They are written up in the final year project report and may be published in a research paper. You can ask us for a short summary of the findings.
